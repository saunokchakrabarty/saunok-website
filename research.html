Firm Entrepreneurship Barriers in India along Caste Lines: Micro-Data and Aggregate Implications

Job Market Paper

I build a quantitative model of firm entrepreneurship in which caste groups differ in worker ability distributions and face caste-specific fixed costs of operating firms. The paper uses this framework to study how barriers to entrepreneurship shape firm ownership, employment allocation, and aggregate output in India.

I discipline the model using the 1998 round of India’s Economic Census, documenting systematic differences in firm size and workforce composition across General, Scheduled Caste, Scheduled Tribe, and Other Backward Classes owned enterprises. Lower-caste-owned firms are smaller on average and employ a higher share of female workers, patterns that persist after controlling for geography, industry, financial access, and formality status.

I use these empirical patterns to quantify the aggregate implications of caste-based entrepreneurship barriers and to evaluate how much misallocation arises from distortions in the ownership and scale of firms.

A Forensic Analysis of US Ad-Valorem and Legislated Tariff Rates, 1909-1933

with Mario Crucini and James Harrison

The US tariff schedules of the early twentieth century are exceptionally complex, often containing more than 5,000 legislatively enumerated tariff lines. These schedules combine ad-valorem duties, specific duties, and compound duties, making it difficult to distinguish legislative changes in protection from changes in effective rates generated by movements in prices. Because specific duties are fixed in nominal terms, volatility in the aggregate ad-valorem-equivalent tariff rate during this period can reflect changes in the nominal price level and relative prices rather than changes in tariff law.

This paper follows the decomposition method of Crucini (1994) to separate line-item ad-valorem-equivalent rates of duty into legislative, price-level, and relative-price components. The resulting tariff history differs sharply from the conventional narrative. The paper constructs a comprehensive panel archive of line-item tariffs and their decompositions, providing a more theoretically grounded foundation for studying the positive and normative effects of interwar US tariff policy.

The Fragmentation of Agricultural Markets in India: Implications for Prices and Welfare

We document substantial deviations from the Law of One Price in India’s agricultural markets and study their implications for prices and welfare. Using a large panel of granular level commodity prices, we measure the extent of spatial price dispersion across markets and examine how this dispersion varies across commodities, states and time. The analysis focuses on whether observed price gaps reflect persistent market fragmentation rather than transitory measurement noise or quality differences.
